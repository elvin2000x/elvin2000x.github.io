# The Worksheets, editable

*From* The Systematic Advantage *by Elvin M. Peters. Appendix D, in reusable form.*

Eleven worksheets, the Plateau Diagnosis included. The book's copy is for working
once. This one is for working every time.

**How to use it.** Copy this whole file into a document per project, or copy a
single worksheet and keep the rest. The blanks are real blanks, so type straight
into them. Nothing here needs an app.

**The browser version.** If you would rather fill these in on screen and download
a clean PDF, they also live at elvinpeters.com/books/the-systematic-advantage/worksheets.
That version is free and needs no email. This file is the one you take with you.

**Current as of August 2026.** The frameworks outlast a model release. Anything
naming a tool, a price, or a capability carries an expiry date, and the online
version above is the one that gets updated.

## The Plateau Diagnosis *(Introduction)*

Take fifteen minutes with this before Chapter 1. There is no correct score; it is a baseline you return to at the end of each Part to track how your practice has shifted. For each dimension, mark the level that most accurately describes your current practice, using specific recent examples rather than idealized behavior.

**Dimension 1: Session Consistency.** *Do your results vary significantly across similar sessions?*
- **Level 1:** Yes, substantially. Same prompts produce different quality regularly, and I can't predict when.
- **Level 2:** Yes, somewhat. Results are inconsistent on complex tasks, more reliable on simple ones.
- **Level 3:** Mostly no. I've reduced variance significantly, but edge cases still behave unpredictably.
- **Level 4:** No. My workflows produce reliably consistent outputs across sessions, and I can explain why when they don't.

**Dimension 2: Context Portability.** *Can you accurately recreate the conditions of your best sessions?*
- **Level 1:** No. I can't reliably locate or recreate what made a past session work well.
- **Level 2:** Sometimes. For my most-used workflows, yes. For anything outside my standard repertoire, no.
- **Level 3:** Usually. I have documentation that covers most of what I do regularly, with gaps.
- **Level 4:** Yes. My context is stored in a form that makes it retrievable and replicable without relying on memory.

**Dimension 3: Workflow Documentation.** *Do you have written descriptions of your recurring AI workflows?*
- **Level 1:** No. My workflows exist in my memory and vary based on what I remember in the moment.
- **Level 2:** Partially. I have some documentation, mainly my prompt library, but the sequence around those prompts is in my head.
- **Level 3:** Mostly. I've documented my highest-priority workflows, though not all of them.
- **Level 4:** Yes. My recurring workflows are written in a form a competent colleague could follow.

**Dimension 4: Delegation Readiness.** *Could a competent colleague run your AI workflows and produce comparable quality?*
- **Level 1:** No. My AI practice is entirely dependent on my knowledge, memory, and judgment.
- **Level 2:** Partially. A colleague could follow my prompts but would struggle with the judgment calls.
- **Level 3:** Mostly. With some coaching, a colleague could run the documented workflows.
- **Level 4:** Yes. My documentation is comprehensive enough for someone else to run my workflows reliably.

**Dimension 5: System vs. Session Orientation.** *When something goes wrong, what do you fix?*
- **Level 1:** The prompt. I adjust it until I get the output I want, then move on.
- **Level 2:** Usually the prompt, occasionally something larger if the problem persists.
- **Level 3:** I often ask whether the workflow design is the problem, though I don't always have a framework for fixing it.
- **Level 4:** The workflow design. I diagnose at the system level before I touch individual prompts.

**My practice is strongest at:** ______________________ **and has the most structural gap at:** ______________________

Where the lowest scores cluster tells you where to read hardest. Low on Workflow Documentation: Chapters 3 and 8. Low on Session Consistency: Chapters 5 and 6. Low across the board: work through Part 1 in full before reading selectively.

## Worksheet 1: The AI Task Matrix *(Ch 2)*

Place one task in a workflow type before you write a prompt for it.

**The task:** ______________________________________________

**Rate the four dimensions.** One means low, three means high. Circle one per row.

| Dimension | What you are rating | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Complexity | Decisions, dependencies, and shifting context | `simple` | | `complex` |
| Consequence | Cost of a wrong output, sized by cost rather than by probability | `a revision` | | `a relationship, money, or reputation` |
| AI-suitability | How well current capability matches what this needs | `poor fit` | | `strong fit` |
| Recurrence | How often the task runs | `rare` | | `every week` |

**Score total (4–12):** ______   **Profile (C / Cq / AI / R):** ___ / ___ / ___ / ___

**Read the score, then read the profile.** Four to six leans Delegation. Seven to nine is a judgment zone: high AI-suitability and recurrence with low complexity and consequence stays in Delegation, while high complexity or consequence moves to Collaboration. Ten to twelve splits by what drives the total: complexity and consequence point to Augmentation, where your judgment is the product; AI-suitability and recurrence point to Collaboration, with a path to Delegation once the workflow matures.

**The three types:** `Delegation` you specify and review · `Collaboration` your judgment stays in the session · `Augmentation` you lead and AI supports.

## Worksheet 6: The Reputational Audit *(Ch 14)*

Run before any high-stakes output leaves your hands. Check each box only when the answer is a genuine yes.

- [ ] **1.** I have identified every Tier 1 claim (figures, dates, names, attributions) and confirmed each against a primary source.
- [ ] **2.** I have traced the reasoning on every Tier 2 conclusion and the logic holds for this situation.
- [ ] **3.** I have weighed every Tier 3 recommendation against my own professional judgment.
- [ ] **4.** If this falls into a high-stakes category, a second set of eyes has reviewed it.
- [ ] **5.** I have considered whether an undetected error would damage a relationship, a client's interests, or the organization's reputation.
- [ ] **6.** I am prepared to put my name on this and answer for it.
