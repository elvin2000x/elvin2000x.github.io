# The 20-Minute Quickstart

*The Systematic Advantage Toolkit*

The book builds a full operating system across sixteen chapters. This gets you a working version of the core in twenty minutes, so you feel the difference today and know what the rest is for. Set a timer. You need one frontier chat tool you already have and access to a couple of your own real files.

## Minute 0 to 5: Build your one-page "about me" file

Open a blank note. Write these five things about your work. Keep it to a page.
1. **Who you are:** your role, your company or clients, your field.
2. **Who you serve:** your audience or stakeholders, and what they care about.
3. **How you sound:** three words for your voice, plus one "never do this."
4. **What you make:** the two or three things you produce most often.
5. **What good looks like:** how you judge a strong output in your work.

This is your portable system instruction. It goes into every tool you use, and it is the single highest-leverage thing in this quickstart.

## Minute 5 to 9: Install it as standing context

Paste that file into your tool's custom-instructions, profile, or project-settings field, wherever the tool keeps context that persists across chats. If your tool has Projects or a similar workspace, make one for your main area of work and put the file there. You have just moved from retyping context every session to having it stand. That is the Memory Hardening Ladder from Chapter 4, one rung up in four minutes.

## Minute 9 to 15: Run one real task with a real brief

Pick a small task you actually need done today. Instead of a bare prompt, give it a five-part brief:
- **Objective:** the one outcome you want.
- **Context:** what the model cannot infer.
- **Constraints:** what it must and must not do, most important first.
- **Format:** the shape you need back.
- **Success:** how you will know it is good.

Send it. Notice the difference from how you prompted before. That gap is the whole book in miniature.

## Minute 15 to 19: Save what worked

Take the brief you just wrote and save it somewhere you will find it: a note, a doc, a prompt-library tool. Give it a name like "client update brief" or "weekly report brief." The next time this task comes up, you start from the asset instead of a blank page. This is Chapter 5's core move: the brief is the asset, the prompt is disposable.

## Minute 19 to 20: Name your next rung

You now have three of the operating system's parts: standing context, a real brief, and one saved asset. Write one line naming what you will add next: another saved brief, a second context file, or a workflow you run often enough to document. That line is your entry point into the rest of the book.

## What you just built

In twenty minutes you moved from prompting a blank tool to directing a small system: context that stands, a brief that carries, and an asset you can reuse. The book scales each of these into the full operating system, adds verification for when the stakes are real, and takes it to your team. You have the seed. The chapters grow it.

---

*Do this once for real work rather than as a demo. The point is a system you keep, an operating layer that outlasts the exercise.*
