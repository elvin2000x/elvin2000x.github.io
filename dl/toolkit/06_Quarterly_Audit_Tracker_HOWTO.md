# Quarterly Practice Audit Tracker: How to Use It

*The Systematic Advantage Toolkit*

The audit in Chapter 16 only compounds if it runs on a cycle and you can see the trend. This tracker holds eight quarters of scores in one place so drift becomes visible. Open the companion `.csv` in any spreadsheet tool.

## The 45-minute quarterly ritual

1. **Score the five dimensions, one to five.** Use the "what a 5 looks like" column as the standard. Write the score in this quarter's column. If you cannot write one honest sentence of evidence for a score, the real score is lower.
2. **Let the average fill in.** In the AVERAGE row, use your spreadsheet's average function across the five dimension cells for that quarter. Watching this line over a year tells you more than any single quarter.
3. **Name your two lowest.** Put them in the "two lowest dimensions" row. These are your focus rather than your strengths. Pouring the quarter into what you are already good at is the plateau's favorite trick.
4. **Write one action per low dimension.** Name the artifact, the timeline, and what done looks like. "Build a verification checklist for financial outputs by week 3" is an action. "Verify more" is not.
5. **Flag stale infrastructure.** Note any system instruction, SOP, or context file that has gone out of date. Flag it here; fix it in the quarter.
6. **Schedule the next audit now.** Put the date in the last row and in your calendar. The cycle is the whole mechanism.

## Reading the trend

- **A dimension stuck at 2 or 3 for two quarters** is where your next real growth is. Read it as a map to your next real growth.
- **An average that stops rising** usually means you have stopped adding infrastructure and are coasting on what you built. That is the signal to run an Architecture Sprint.
- **A dimension that drops** after a model change or a job change is normal. Calibration ages. That is what the quarterly cycle catches.

## Make a chart (optional, two minutes)

Select the dimension rows and the quarter columns, insert a line chart, and you have a picture of your practice over time. The line going up is the book working. A flat line is the invitation to build the next layer.
