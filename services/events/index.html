01

What the room walks out with

A talk has one job, which is to change what people do the following week. Everything in the session points at that, and the measure of whether it worked is what shows up in their calendars on Monday.

  • A method they can use the same day, taught once and then demonstrated live on a real task rather than described in the abstract
  • Real numbers from real systems, what the work cost and what it produced, so nobody has to take a claim on faith
  • The failures alongside the wins, because a room that only hears success stories leaves believing something untrue
  • A one page method card, handed out or delivered by QR code, that outlives the event
  • One specific action for the week ahead, named from the stage so it is impossible to leave without it
  • Straight answers in Q and A, including the times when the honest answer is that AI is the wrong tool for what they just described

If what you want is people building something during the session rather than listening to somebody who has, that is a workshop and it is a different product. The two sit side by side further down this page.

04

Events this is built for

The best fit is an audience that has already tried AI and came away underwhelmed. Somebody signed everyone up for the tools, two or three curious people quietly got good at them, and the rest of the organization carried on working the slow way. That room does not need to be told AI is important. It needs somebody who has done the work to show them what good looks like.

  • Business conferences and industry events that want a practitioner session instead of another trends keynote
  • Company townhalls and all-hands where leadership wants everybody hearing the same thing on the same day
  • Team and company offsites that need one session with substance between the strategy blocks
  • Panels and fireside chats that need somebody who will answer the question that was asked
  • Association and chapter meetings, professional bodies and membership organizations
  • Lunch and learns, the lowest-friction way to put a real AI session in front of your team
  • Leadership and board sessions where the questions are about risk, cost and what to standardize

It is a poor fit for engineering audiences who want model architecture and fine-tuning, and for any event where the speaker is expected to promote a vendor. I sell no software and I have no partner to plug, which is a large part of why these sessions are useful.

05

An event talk or a workshop

These are two different products, and booking the wrong one is the most common way this goes sideways. The question that settles it is whether the room listens or the room builds.

Book an event talk when

The audience is large, the time is short, the session sits inside a bigger agenda, and the goal is to move how a lot of people think on the same day. A talk scales without limit. Nobody needs a laptop. What you get is shared understanding plus one clear action.

Book a workshop when

You want people leaving with something they built themselves. A workshop runs on your team's own tasks, collected beforehand, with everyone working on their own laptop while I move around the room. It takes longer, it caps the group size so that moving around the room is possible at all, and what you get is working prompts, a written team standard and a real task that runs better on Monday.

How to book

Six steps, and the only one that needs your calendar today is the first.

  1. Send a note, then we talk for twenty minutes. We cover the event, the audience, the theme, the one thing you want people doing differently afterwards, and your budget and approval process. No pitch and no deck.
  2. A one page proposal arrives within 48 hours of the call. The outline, the format, one price and the terms, written so you can forward it to whoever signs off without having to explain it yourself.
  3. You confirm and I invoice the deposit. 50% holds the date and the balance is due 15 days after the event. If your organization needs vendor onboarding, a purchase order, insurance documentation or a supplier form, send it over and it comes back the same week.
  4. You get the promotion pack the same week. Session title, abstract, bio and headshot, ready for your event page and your invitations, so your marketing is never waiting on me.
  5. I build your talk. Examples rebuilt around your industry, demos rehearsed live at real speed, and a tech check with your AV team before the day rather than during it.
  6. We run it, then we follow up. The method card goes out to attendees, unanswered questions get written answers, and you get a straight read on what your organization should do next, including the times when the answer is nothing more from me.

Frequently asked questions

How far ahead do we need to book?

Three to six weeks is comfortable for an internal event such as a townhall or a lunch and learn. Conferences and association programs usually set their lineup three to six months ahead. If your date is sooner than three weeks, ask, because it is often still possible.

How big can the audience be?

Any size. A talk scales in a way a hands-on workshop does not, so a room of 500 works as well as a room of 30. If you want people building something during the session rather than listening, that is the workshop instead.

Do you speak remotely?

Yes. In person across the Greater Toronto Area with travel included in the fee, and live over video anywhere. For virtual events the talk gets rebuilt for video with shorter segments and live audience interaction, rather than delivered as a webcam version of the in-person talk.

Do you customize the talk to our event?

Always. Before the date we agree the theme, the audience, and the one thing you want people to do differently afterwards. The examples get rebuilt around your industry, and for internal events a short survey of attendees shapes what gets covered.

Have you spoken at events before?

Event speaking is a new line for me and I would rather say so than imply a client list I do not have. What sits behind the material is a decade in marketing presenting to senior stakeholders and executive teams, a published book on the method, and AI systems I run in production every day. The guarantee exists precisely because you are taking a chance on a reference list I am still building, and I do not think you should have to carry that risk.

Are your fees in Canadian dollars?

Yes. All fees are Canadian dollars plus HST, invoiced from an incorporated Ontario business with a GST/HST registration number. Travel inside the Greater Toronto Area is included. Outside the GTA, travel and accommodation are billed at cost and agreed in writing first.

Can we record the session and reuse it?

Recording for internal use by people who could not attend is included. Putting the recording into a permanent training library or anything customer-facing is a separate licence, agreed in writing before the date.

What do you need from our AV team?

A screen or projector with HDMI, a microphone in any room over 40 people, and fifteen minutes before doors to test with my own laptop. For virtual events I need a tech check on the actual platform a few days ahead, which is when problems are still cheap to fix.

Do you promote products or vendors from the stage?

No. I sell no software and I hold no vendor partnerships, so there is nothing for me to push. The book gets a single mention and only where it is relevant, and I am happy to drop even that if you would rather I did not.

Let us talk about your event.

Twenty minutes is enough to scope the session, the format and the fee. If I am not the right speaker for your audience, I will say so on the call and point you somewhere better.
