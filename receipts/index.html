Proof

One review, no case studies, and the most checkable numbers in this category.

I have one Amazon review and no client case studies. Rather than write vague quotes to fill the gap, here is what I can actually prove: what each system cost to build and run, logged per call at the time of the call, and what it produced. Including the parts that did not work, which is the half nobody publishes.

What it cost

Every number here was logged at the time, not reconstructed after

Costs were written per API call as the call happened, with real token counts. That makes these receipts rather than estimates, and it is the reason I am willing to print them next to my own failures.

  • $32.67
    293,571 words across 210 pieces, in twenty-five days

    Eleven cents per thousand words. Essays, short stories and columns, drafted, voice-scrubbed and machine-rated before a human ever saw them.

    Source: per-run generation ledger, cost and word fields summed over the runs array
  • $0.32
    446 short posts generated

    0.073 cents each. Written per call at call time with real token counts, which makes it a receipt file rather than an estimate.

    Source: per-call token log, 823 calls between 2026-05-28 and 2026-07-09
  • $0.00
    7,729 business listings across 15 live directories

    445 scraper runs and 13.79 hours of compute. 35,564 raw records collected, 15,295 passed the quality bar. No language model anywhere in that pipeline, which is why it cost nothing.

    Source: the 15 listings files, the scraper logs, and 102 run summaries
  • $0.00
    3,384 static SEO pages built and published

    Plain string interpolation, no API library anywhere in it. The honest caveat, printed here because it belongs next to the number: 600 of those pages come from 20 unique bodies. Real surface area, not 600 distinct pieces of writing.

    Source: the two page-generation engines and their output directories
What broke

The half nobody publishes

Every one of these is measured, sourced and unflattering. They are here because a cost receipt is only believable next to the record of what the cheap output was actually worth.

  • 100% Of the first 200 pieces, every title began with the word “The”.

    Not sampled. Counted. Read one at a time they were fine, which is exactly why I did not notice for a month.

  • 22 of 78 One character name, across the short-story set.

    Asked for a name with no further instruction, the model returned something plausible, and plausible has a shape. Recounted against the story files in the 2026-08-11 audit: 22 of 78 starred a Marina, and two stories opened with nearly the same sentence.

  • 0.0 Median engagement across the 113-entry posting log (100 published).

    The best single post got two interactions. By then the writing had genuinely improved, which is the uncomfortable part: quality was never the bottleneck, distribution was.

  • ~50% Of AI-generated ad images shipped with garbled text.

    Measured on my own output. It is why the current pipeline renders text in CSS and screenshots it instead of asking a model to draw letters.
