What is actually different about marketing in Vancouver

Vancouver is the third largest market in Canada and one of the most fragmented. The city itself is a fraction of the metro area: most of the people who would describe themselves as Vancouver customers live in Burnaby, Richmond, Surrey, Coquitlam, North Vancouver, Langley and Delta, in municipalities with their own downtowns and their own competitors. A campaign targeted at the City of Vancouver looks tidy on a map and quietly excludes most of the buyers you were trying to reach.

The second thing that separates Vancouver is cost. Rent is higher, wages are higher, and the businesses bidding against you are carrying the same overhead, which pushes what they are willing to pay per click. That does not make advertising unaffordable, it makes waste more expensive. Every month a campaign runs on loose match types in this market costs you more than the same mistake would cost in Winnipeg or London.

The practical consequence is that Vancouver rewards precision and punishes patience. I would rather set a tight budget with a conversion action that counts booked work, prove the unit economics inside a quarter, and then scale what already pays. That is the opposite of the usual advice to give a campaign six months to find its feet, and in this market the usual advice is expensive.

Questions Vancouver businesses ask

Do you have an office in Vancouver?

No. I am based in Toronto and work from home, so there is no Vancouver office and I am not going to imply there is. Everything is run remotely over video and email, which is how I run every account. If you want someone who will walk into your showroom, I am the wrong hire and I will tell you that on the call.

Does the time difference matter?

Three hours, and it matters for meetings rather than for the work. I take Vancouver calls in your morning, which is my early afternoon, and the accounts themselves do not care what time zone I am in. If you need someone reachable at 4pm Pacific on short notice, say so up front and we will agree what that looks like.

Should we target the City of Vancouver or all of Metro Vancouver?

Almost always wider than the city, and the exact answer depends on how far your customers will travel for what you sell. A restaurant is a neighbourhood business. A trade, a clinic or a B2B service usually has customers across Burnaby, Richmond, Surrey, Coquitlam, North Vancouver, Langley and Delta and is leaving money on the table by targeting the city alone. This is the first thing I look at in a Vancouver account.

Is Vancouver more expensive to advertise in than the rest of Canada?

Generally yes, in the sense that your competitors have high fixed costs and bid accordingly, and in real estate and construction, film and television production, technology, international trade and tourism and hospitality particularly. That is an argument for tighter targeting and better conversion tracking, not an argument against advertising. I would rather run a disciplined $3,000 a month here than a loose $10,000.
