What is actually different about marketing across Saskatchewan

Almost everywhere else in Canada, province-wide targeting is an expensive default, because one dominant expensive city ends up setting the price for every smaller market in the geography. Saskatchewan is the exception. Saskatoon and Regina are close enough in size and cost that neither distorts the other, and in agriculture and agtech, potash and uranium mining, energy, manufacturing and professional services the customer base genuinely is provincial rather than local. Here, drawing a tight radius around your own city is the more common mistake.

That customer base also extends past the border more often than owners assume. Agtech, mining supply and specialist professional firms based here routinely sell into Alberta, Manitoba, North Dakota and further, while their advertising is set to a circle around the office. Getting the geography right is usually worth more than getting the ad copy right.

The province grew 3.1% between the last two censuses, the slowest growth outside Atlantic Canada, and that is worth saying plainly. It means the market is not going to hand you customers through population growth, so gains have to come from taking share. The thin competitive field is what makes that realistic: very few Saskatchewan businesses have published anything that seriously competes for a search result.

Questions Saskatchewan businesses ask

Do you have an office in Saskatchewan?

No. I am based in Toronto and work from home, so there is no Saskatchewan office and I am not going to imply there is. Everything is run remotely over video and email, which is how I run every account.

Should we target the whole province or just our city?

In Saskatchewan, usually the whole province, which is the opposite of my advice in Ontario or British Columbia. Saskatoon and Regina are close enough in cost that neither inflates the other, and in most of the industries here the customers are spread across the province rather than concentrated in one city.

Is there enough search volume to justify paid ads?

Sometimes not, and I would rather look than guess. In smaller Saskatchewan categories the honest answer is that SEO and AIO are worth doing and Google Ads is not, and I will tell you that on the call rather than selling you all three by default.

Can you help us sell outside Saskatchewan?

Yes, and for agtech, mining supply and remote-capable professional firms it is often the biggest growth available. The plan changes, because outside the province you compete on expertise rather than proximity, and that is a content and AI visibility problem more than an advertising one.

What does this cost?

Google Ads management is $1,500 a month plus your ad spend. SEO consulting is $1,200 a month. AIO consulting is $1,800 one time. No setup fees and no long contract.
