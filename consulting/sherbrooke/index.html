What is actually different about marketing in Sherbrooke

Sherbrooke operates in French, and any plan that does not start there is wrong. What makes this city different from Trois-Rivières or Saguenay is that the English side is not negligible either. Bishop's University teaches in English in Lennoxville, the Eastern Townships have a long-established anglophone community, and the Université de Sherbrooke brings in students and researchers from outside the province. For a lot of businesses here the correct answer is a French campaign with a genuine English one beside it, rather than the French-only default that is right almost everywhere else outside Montreal.

The universities distort the market in a second way that matters more than the language question for some businesses. A significant share of the people counted in Sherbrooke are students, which means the census figure overstates the buying market for anything expensive and understates the seasonality of everything else. Demand here moves on the academic calendar, and a business judged on a thirty-day window that crosses September or May is being judged on a change that had nothing to do with its marketing.

Sherbrooke grew 7.2% between the last two censuses against 5.2% nationally, so there is genuine expansion underneath all of that. The competitive field is thin by the standards of Montreal or Quebec City, which makes organic positions and AI recommendations realistically winnable here in a way they are not in the two big Quebec markets.

Questions Sherbrooke businesses ask

Do you have an office in Sherbrooke?

No. I am based in Toronto and work from home, so there is no Sherbrooke office and I am not going to imply there is. Everything is run remotely over video and email, which is how I run every account.

Do you work in French?

I can build and run the French side of a campaign, and here is the limit stated plainly: I am not a native French speaker, so French copy I write should be reviewed by someone who is before it goes live. In a market that operates in French I would rather budget for that review than pretend it is optional. If you have a francophone on staff, that is usually the best and cheapest answer.

Should we advertise in English at all?

In most of Quebec I would say no. In Sherbrooke it depends on your customers, and for businesses touching Bishop's, the anglophone Townships community or the research community it is often worth a small separate campaign. The test is whether you can name English-speaking customers you already have, not whether English feels like a bigger market in the abstract.

How do the universities affect our numbers?

They inflate the population figure relative to the people who can actually buy an expensive service, and they put a hard seasonal shape on demand. If your year-over-year looks strange every September and May, that is usually the calendar rather than the campaign, and it is worth checking before anyone changes anything.

What about the language requirements for commercial signage and advertising?

Quebec's language legislation applies to commercial communications and that includes advertising. I am not a lawyer and nothing here is legal advice. The practical position I take is to build French-first as the default and point clients to the Office québécois de la langue française for the actual requirements rather than guessing at them.
