What is actually different about marketing in Halifax

Halifax is the regional capital for four provinces. That is the fact that should shape your marketing, because it means the customer base is spread across a large area and low density, and it means your competitors are as likely to be in Moncton or St. John's as across the harbour. Targeting a radius around Halifax makes sense for a restaurant and makes almost no sense for a professional services firm, a distributor or anything sold remotely.

The second thing is that the region punches above its weight in specific sectors. Ocean industries, defence, post-secondary research and health care are concentrated here in a way that creates real B2B and institutional demand, and those buyers do not come through a search ad. They come through being visible and credible while somebody researches, which is SEO and AIO work rather than paid.

The third is seasonality. Tourism and the student calendar move a lot of local demand, and a business that spreads its budget evenly across the year is underspending in the months that actually pay. That is easy to fix and it is one of the more common things I find in Halifax accounts.

Questions Halifax businesses ask

Do you have an office in Halifax?

No. I am based in Toronto and work from home, so there is no Halifax office and I am not going to imply there is. Everything is run remotely over video and email, which is how I run every account. The one hour time difference is worth noting and it has never been a problem.

Should we advertise across Atlantic Canada or just Halifax?

It depends entirely on what you sell and whether you can deliver it at a distance. A trade or a clinic should stay local. A professional services firm, a distributor, or anything delivered remotely is usually leaving money on the table by stopping at the municipal boundary, because the rest of the region has thin competition and searchers who currently find nobody local.

Which service should a Halifax business start with?

If you need customers this quarter and you sell to households, Google Ads. If you sell to institutions, government or other businesses, SEO and AIO, because those buyers research long before they contact anybody. Pick one and do it properly.

Our business is seasonal. How should we budget?

Concentrate the ad spend in and just ahead of your season, keep a small presence through the quiet months so you are not restarting from zero, and use the slow period to build the SEO that will be ranking when demand returns. Flat monthly budgets are the most common waste I find in seasonal accounts.
