See the difference

The same task, two very different results.

One person asked AI for help. The other gave it a brief, the way you would brief a sharp new hire.

Asked10 minutes

“Write me an email to a client about a project delay.”

A generic draft in the wrong tone. They rewrote the whole thing.

Directed2 minutes

“Client delay email. Two weeks late. Client values transparency. Tone: collaborative, not apologetic. Under 150 words. New date: March 15.”

Right voice and right length. One minute of polish, then sent.

Three more examples
AskedA shorter pile of notes

“Summarize these meeting notes.”

No decisions, no owners, nothing anyone can act on.

DirectedA follow-up, sent the same day

“From these notes: decisions made, action items with owner and date, open questions. Three lists, nothing else.”

The meeting produces something.

AskedYour own columns, read back

“What does this data tell me?”

A description where you needed a decision.

DirectedA briefing you can defend

“Act as an analyst briefing a skeptical CFO. Name the three changes that matter, why each happened, and what the numbers cannot explain.”

Findings, causes and the honest gaps.

AskedA posting like every other posting

“Write a job description for a marketing manager.”

Buzzwords and no reason to apply.

DirectedA posting people answer

“Job posting, marketing manager, 12-person B2B firm. Lead with the problems this person owns in month one. Plain language, under 400 words.”

Specific and honest about the work.

Both people had the same tool. The second one had learned to direct it, and that is the skill this book teaches.

About the author

Elvin M. Peters

Author of the AI Fluency Series · Toronto

My first lesson in directing machines came at a large Canadian fintech. I ran over $12M a year in paid media there, and I cut the cost per funded loan by more than half by teaching the bidding algorithms what a good customer looked like.

Then an adtech giant building AI brought me in to advise the media teams at big advertisers, where AI cut my account audits from hours to minutes. On both sides of the table I met capable people getting a fraction of what AI could give them. I wrote this book for them.

The Artificial Advantage is book one of the AI Fluency Series. Book two, The Systematic Advantage, is in progress. Find me on LinkedIn.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a technical background?

None. The book is written for professionals with no technical training. If you can write an email, you can use everything in it.

Which AI tool does it use?

It works with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot. Part 2 helps you pick one, and the directing skill carries over to all of them.

Will it go out of date when the tools change?

The book teaches you how to direct AI, and that skill carries over from one model to the next. The product names will keep changing, and your briefs will keep working.

I already use AI at work. Is it still for me?

Yes, if your results are hit and miss. The frameworks turn scattered habits into a method you can repeat, and the appendices give you prompts and fixes to keep beside your work.

Which format should I get?

Kindle, paperback and hardcover are all on Amazon, with Amazon's standard returns. The paperback is handy to keep open beside your desk for the appendices.

Can I read some of it first?

Yes. Chapter 8 is free to read, and it shows you which AI mode fits which job.
