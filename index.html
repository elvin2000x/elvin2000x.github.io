The Real Reason It Felt Like A Gimmick

You've used AI. So why does it still feel underwhelming?

  • You asked a question and got a generic answer.
  • It hedged, padded the reply, or got a fact wrong.
  • You rewrote it yourself and decided the hype was overblown.

Professionals who get great results type into the same box you do.

They learned one skill nobody taught you, which is how to direct the tool. That skill is the whole gap, and you can learn it.

Why This Book Exists

I watched the gap widen every single day inside big tech.

At a large Canadian fintech I ran an average of $1.2M a month in paid media myself for three years, across two regulated consumer-lending brands. Then an adtech giant that is building AI brought me in to advise the media teams at big advertisers, and the same pattern showed up on both sides of the table. Highly capable professionals were using AI every day and pulling a fraction of what it could deliver. The reason was never intelligence. Effective AI direction is a skill, and no one had handed them a framework for it.

The whole book rests on one idea: stop asking AI, start directing it. Here is what that looks like with the same task and two different people:

Without a framework
“Write me an email to a client about a project delay.”
10 minutesGeneric output, wrong tone. They rewrote the whole thing.
With direction
“Client delay email. Two weeks late. Client values transparency. Tone: collaborative, not apologetic. Under 150 words. New date: March 15.”
2 minutesRight voice, right tone, right length. One minute of polish. Sent.
Three more, same idea
Without a framework
“Summarize these meeting notes.”
A shorter pile of notesNo decisions, no owners, nothing anyone can act on.
With direction
“From these notes: decisions made, action items with owner and date, open questions. Three lists, nothing else.”
A follow-up, sent same dayThe meeting actually produces something.
Without a framework
“What does this data tell me?”
Your own columns, read backA description where you needed a decision.
With direction
“Act as an analyst briefing a skeptical CFO. Name the three changes that matter, why each happened, and what the numbers cannot explain.”
A briefing you can defendFindings, causes, and the honest gaps.
Without a framework
“Write a job description for a marketing manager.”
A posting like every other postingBuzzwords, nothing specific, no reason to apply.
With direction
“Job posting, marketing manager, 12-person B2B firm. Lead with the problems this person owns in month one. Plain language, under 400 words.”
A posting people answerSpecific, honest about the work.

Same tool. Same task. Same professional context. The only difference was AI literacy.

The professionals getting exceptional results were not more technical. They were more deliberate.

I wrote this book because I could not find the one I wished I had.
