The receipts this was built from

Almost everything written about AI content marketing is a projection. This is a record. I ran a writing machine at full speed for a month, logged every call with its real cost, then audited the output by counting rather than by reading. These four numbers are what came back.

That is the finding underneath all the others, and it changes what the problem is. When producing a thousand words costs eleven cents, volume stops being the constraint and judgement becomes the whole game. This guide is built around that.

What a firehose produces

Every piece passed a read-through. I read them as they came out, one at a time, and each one was fine. The problem was invisible at the level of the piece and obvious at the level of the pile, and nobody reads their own pile.

What I thought I had
Two hundred pieces in a month. Essays, short stories and columns. Competent, well-formed, on topic, and produced for the price of a sandwich.
A libraryOr so it looked from inside, one piece at a time, which is the only way I ever looked at it.
What the count found
Every title opened with “The”. One character name in 21 of 75 stories. A death in 69% of them, a mother in 75%, grief in 52%. Two opened with nearly the same sentence.
One piece, 200 timesNot a body of work. A single story with two hundred different titles on it, all of which began the same way.

Then it got better, and it still reached nobody. The writing was no longer the problem by then. There was simply nobody in the room, because I had built a machine for producing and none for reaching anyone.

Both failures have a fix, both fixes are components, and both components are in this guide. That is the honest reason it exists: I paid for this education by making the mistakes at volume, with the logs running.

The five components

The machine is five things. None of them needs code, an API key, or a subscription beyond the AI tool you already pay for. You build them once, in an afternoon, and then they do their job every time you produce anything.

One

The brief

The document the machine reads before every piece: who you are, who reads you, which facts are approved, and what you will never claim. Almost every complaint about generic AI writing is a missing brief.

Two

The rotation

A spreadsheet that hands the machine a different combination of topic, angle, shape and title form every time. This is the component I did not have, and its absence is why all two hundred titles matched.

Three

The voice rules

A banned list a stranger could apply without knowing you, plus three positive rules. Taste is not enforceable. A list is. The rules go into the prompt, where they prevent, rather than into the edit, where they only scrub.

Four

The gate

A five-dimension rubric with a threshold, scored in a separate chat before you read the piece. One of the five dimensions cannot be judged on a single piece at all, and it is the one that catches sameness.

Five

The queue

A tab and three folders that keep a person in front of the publish button. Nothing in this system publishes itself, and editing an approved piece sends it back to draft.

And then

You run it

Ten pieces to prove it works, a thirty-minute weekly loop, the distribution step that decides whether any of it reaches a person, and a ledger that tells you the truth about all three.

Who this is for

Yes, if

You need to publish regularly and you are one person, or close to it. You have tried asking AI for content and been quietly unimpressed by what came back. You would rather build something once than negotiate with a blank page every week. You are willing to reject your own work sometimes.

Probably not, if

You want a tool that posts for you while you sleep. You are looking for the prompt that fixes everything. You publish once a quarter, in which case the machine costs more to build than it saves. Or you want the Python version: this edition is deliberately no-code, and the self-hosted one is a separate product not yet written.

What this is not

Being clear about this up front is cheaper for both of us than a refund.

  • Not a promise of a thousand posts. No system of mine produced a thousand of anything, and any page that told you otherwise would be selling you a number rather than a method.
  • Not software. You get a guide, and you build the machine yourself out of a spreadsheet, some documents, and whatever AI tool you already use. Nothing to install, nothing to subscribe to, nothing that breaks when a vendor changes an interface.
  • Not a growth-hack playbook. The distribution step is three rules and a warning, drawn from watching decent writing reach nobody. It will not get you an audience by Friday.
  • Not an argument that AI writing is fine as it is. Half of this guide is a record of what it produces when you leave it unsupervised, which is exactly why the other half is built the way it is.
  • Not written by the machine. The guide was drafted, cut and rewritten by hand, which is the least I could do given the subject.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which AI tool do I need?

Whichever one you already use. The five components are documents and a spreadsheet, and the prompts are plain text with no tool-specific syntax. I run mine on Claude, and nothing in the guide depends on that.

How long before it is running?

An afternoon to build all five components, and about two hours for your first ten pieces. After that it is roughly ten minutes per piece and thirty minutes a week of maintenance.

Is this going to make my writing sound like everyone else's AI?

That is the failure the whole guide is organised against. Step 3 gives you a banned list of the specific tells readers recognise, and Step 4 scores every piece against your last twenty for distinctness before you ever see it.

Do I need to be technical?

No. This edition is deliberately no-code: a spreadsheet, a few documents, and copy-paste. I built my own version in Python, and that is described as background rather than as something you have to reproduce.

What is the difference between this and The Claude Operator's Manual?

The manual teaches one tool in depth: projects, skills, connectors, the API, Claude Code. This teaches one job: producing publishable volume, on any tool. They overlap almost nowhere, and plenty of people will want both.

Will there be a version with the code in it?

Probably, as a separate product. This edition covers the method, which is the part that transfers. The Python implementation is specific to my machine and my file paths, and shipping it as though it were plug-and-play would not be honest.
