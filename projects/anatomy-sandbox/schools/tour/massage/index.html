Massage therapy: what sits under your hands

About 5 minutes · 5 stops

Learning outcome: Students can find the fascia, back and neck muscles and the sciatic nerve they work around, and open a stretch to coach for the neck.

1. Fascia over the thigh

Open the app

Do: In the Body tab under Body systems, turn on Fascia, then search Fascia lata and pick it.

You see: Fascia shows as a white see-through film.

Fact: The tensor fasciae latae is a small muscle at the front edge of the hip bone whose job is to tighten the fascia lata. (source)

2. The upper back

Do: Search traps, pick the part of the trapezius that runs up to the neck, and press Back.

You see: The trapezius spreads from the skull and neck down the middle of the back and out to the shoulder blades.

Fact: The trapezius is the most superficial of all the back muscles. (source)

3. A neck stretch to coach

Side Neck Stretch

You see: The camera flies to the neck muscles this stretch targets, and the card lists the steps you'd coach.

Fact: The levator scapulae runs from the first four neck vertebrae down to the inner border of the shoulder blade. (source)

4. The long back muscles

Do: Press Clear, search erectors, pick Longissimus thoracis muscle and press Back.

You see: The long muscles of the lower back run up both sides of the spine, and clicking each one names it.

Fact: The erector spinae sits on each side of the spine, between the spinous processes and the angles of the ribs. (source)

5. Piriformis and the sciatic nerve

Do: Search Piriformis and pick the muscle, then search Sciatic nerve and pick it.

You see: The thick yellow sciatic nerve leaves the pelvis just below the piriformis and heads down the back of the thigh.

Fact: The sciatic nerve enters the buttock directly below the piriformis, so the piriformis is the landmark used to find it. (source)
