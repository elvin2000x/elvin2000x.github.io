What your students do with it

Anatomy Sandbox is built on the open Z-Anatomy atlas: 4,080 parts across 11 layers, from skin and fascia down to bones, nerves, organs and lymph. Students click any part to see its name, or search in plain gym words like "quads" and "rotator cuff".

They can also play a real exercise rep, like a squat or a sit-to-stand, and watch each muscle turn red as it shortens and blue as it stretches. The colours show change in length only, so they don't measure effort or force.

Common questions

What does the pilot cost, and what happens on April 30?

Nothing. The pilot is free for your whole campus until April 30, 2027, with no card and no contract. You tick one box to accept the pilot terms: classroom mode on, anonymous usage counts and two short check-ins. Nothing renews or bills on its own, so doing nothing ends it. If you want to keep it, sign by May 31, 2027 and CA$390 a year is locked for 3 years (list CA$790), or one teacher can keep it for CA$190 a year.

Do we have to install anything?

No. It runs in the browser with nothing to install. If your IT team wants it on every computer or offline, it's one policy and about 5 minutes of IT setup on Chromebooks, Group Policy or iPads (under 10 with Intune), and the IT setup page has the steps for Google Admin, Intune and Group Policy, plus an offline file for labs with no internet.

Will it run on our Chromebooks and our Wi-Fi?

The quickest way to know is to open it now on one of your own Chromebooks, with no form. The model is about 27 MB, and after the first open it's saved on the device, so later classes load it from the device instead of your network. A lighter first view for older machines is planned.

What about reproductive and genital anatomy?

On your campus link it's hidden when the app opens. Only a teacher with your campus PIN can show it, it hides again when the page reloads, and students can't get around it by editing the link. Your program decides when those systems are taught.

What do you collect about students?

No names, no emails and no accounts. There's no sign-in and no cookie, and our classroom service stores no IP addresses. The page itself is served by GitHub Pages, and the model files come from our own server in Helsinki, whose standard logs are kept for a short period. Your campus link counts how many times it was opened each day, with no names attached, so you can see it's being used. Everything loads from our own server. The offline file loads nothing from anywhere. The privacy page has the full data flow for your review.

Is it accessible?

We're building to WCAG 2.1 AA: a full keyboard path, a searchable text list of every part as the alternative to the 3D view, labelled controls, contrast and reduced motion. Our Accessibility Conformance Report will state any gaps plainly, and it's ready by October 25.

How does it fit our program?

Three lesson packs are included, each with 5 activities and a printable answer sheet: PSW body systems, fitness program muscle ID, and Grade 12 kinesiology (PSK4U B1.1 and B1.2). Massage therapy, physio assistant and biology programs get a free 5-minute walkthrough that opens the app at each stop. Every exercise also has its own link, which an instructor can paste into Brightspace or Google Classroom.

Who made the anatomy, and is it accurate?

The body comes from Z-Anatomy, an open 3D atlas, with female organs from the NIH Human Reference Atlas and 876 exercises from free-exercise-db, all credited on the credits page. Lesson-pack answers are checked against OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e and TeachMeAnatomy. Red and blue during a rep show a muscle shortening or stretching, and they don't show effort or force.

How is it different from Visible Body or Complete Anatomy?

Students press Play on a real exercise and watch each muscle shorten and stretch, and every muscle links to the exercises that train it and the stretches for it. There are no student accounts, and the price is published: one campus licence covers every student. It also runs fine beside a tool you already have.

The offline file runs on your own computers with no connection to us, and the model data is open-licence and credited, so your campus never depends on a sales rep.

Not yet. The interface and labels are in English for this pilot.
