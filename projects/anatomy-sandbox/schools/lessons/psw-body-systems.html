PSW Body Systems

For: Personal Support Worker programs (public colleges, career colleges, school board continuing education) · 85 minutes in total, or one activity per class

Five short activities that tie each body system to a task a PSW does on shift: helping a client stand, taking a pulse, measuring output, checking the skin. Students work in the browser. There is nothing to install and no login.

Curriculum fit. PSW Program Standard (January 2022), Vocational Learning Outcome 8, element C: "Identify and apply knowledge of the basic structure and function of all human body systems to the routine activities of daily living." Element H: "Use proper body mechanics, assistive devices and techniques to safely position, transfer, move and ambulate clients."

Source: PSW Program Standard (January 2022), VLO 8, element C, p. 28

Before class

  1. Open https://elvinpeters.com/projects/anatomy-sandbox/ on a classroom computer once, so you know how long it takes to load on your network. It opens on the skeleton.
  2. Click through each activity link. Each one opens the app in the right place.
  3. Print the student sheets (this page, from the Print button) and keep the answer sheet (psw-body-systems-answers.html) for yourself.

Activities

  1. Bones, falls and fractures (Musculoskeletal, 15 min)
  2. Sit-to-stand and safe transfers (Musculoskeletal (body mechanics), 15 min)
  3. Heart, lungs and breathing (Cardiovascular and respiratory, 20 min)
  4. Digestion, urine and elimination care (Digestive and urinary, 20 min)
  5. Skin and pressure injuries (Integumentary, 15 min)

Controls students will use

  1. Drag to turn the body, scroll or pinch to zoom, click any part to see its name.
  2. The search box ("Search structures") finds any part by name and flies to it.
  3. The Body tab holds Body systems (turn layers on and off), Colour and Cut.
  4. The Front, Back, Left, Right and Top buttons set the view. On an exercise, Play rep runs the movement.

Activity 1. Bones, falls and fractures

Musculoskeletal · about 15 minutes · Name: ____________________

Open

  • Open the skeletonhttps://elvinpeters.com/projects/anatomy-sandbox/

Do

  1. Open the link. The app starts on the skeleton.
  2. Click the search box (it says "Search structures") and type each name below. Pick the result to fly to it: Femur, Hip bone, Sacrum, Vertebra L3, Radius, Ulna, Calcaneus.
  3. Use the Front and Back view buttons to look at each bone from both sides. Drag to turn the body, scroll to zoom.

Answer

  1. An older client falls and the nurse says "hip fracture". Which bone is broken, and at which end?
  2. A client falls onto an outstretched hand. Which forearm bone most often breaks near the wrist?
  3. Standing in the anatomical position, which forearm bone sits on the thumb side?
  4. Name two jobs the skeleton does besides holding the body up.

Activity 2. Sit-to-stand and safe transfers

Musculoskeletal (body mechanics) · about 15 minutes · Name: ____________________

Open

  • Chair Squat (sit-to-stand)https://elvinpeters.com/projects/anatomy-sandbox/#ex=Chair_Squat
  • Back extension (lower back muscles)https://elvinpeters.com/projects/anatomy-sandbox/#ex=Hyperextensions_Back_Extensions

Do

  1. Open the Chair Squat link. The main muscles light up strong red and the ones that help light up soft orange.
  2. Press Play rep to watch the body sit and stand. This is the same movement a client makes getting out of a chair.
  3. Click the front of the thigh and then the buttock to see each muscle's name.
  4. Open the Back extension link to see the muscles along the lower spine.

Answer

  1. Which muscle group straightens the knee as a client stands up? Name its four muscles.
  2. Gluteus maximus is the main muscle that extends the hip. When does the body call on it most?
  3. Which muscle group runs along the spine and extends it? Name its three parts.
  4. List three body mechanics habits you use when you help move a client.

Activity 3. Heart, lungs and breathing

Cardiovascular and respiratory · about 20 minutes · Name: ____________________

Open

  • Open the apphttps://elvinpeters.com/projects/anatomy-sandbox/

Do

  1. Open the link, then open the Body tab and find Body systems.
  2. Turn on Heart & vessels and Organs. Turn off Bones if the ribs block the view. Give the vessels a few seconds to load before you search for them.
  3. Search and pick each one: Left ventricle, Right ventricle, Ascending aorta, Trachea, Superior lobe of right lung, Middle lobe of right lung, Diaphragm, Radial artery.
  4. Under Cut, press Coronal and move the slider to look inside the chest.

Answer

  1. Which heart chamber pumps blood out to the whole body, and into which artery?
  2. Where does the right ventricle send its blood?
  3. How many lobes does each lung have? (Search "lobe of right lung" and "lobe of left lung" to check.)
  4. What does the diaphragm do when a client breathes in?
  5. Where do you feel a radial pulse?

Activity 4. Digestion, urine and elimination care

Digestive and urinary · about 20 minutes · Name: ____________________

Open

  • Open the apphttps://elvinpeters.com/projects/anatomy-sandbox/
  • Female bodyhttps://elvinpeters.com/projects/anatomy-sandbox/#sex=f

Do

  1. Open the first link. In Body systems, turn on Organs.
  2. Search and pick each one: Oesophagus, Stomach, Duodenum, Jejunum, Ascending colon, Transverse colon, Descending colon, Sigmoid colon, Liver, Kidney, Ureter, Urinary bladder.
  3. Open the Female body link, turn on Organs, and search "Female urethra". Use the Male and Female buttons in the header to compare it with the male "Urethra".

Teacher note. The Female body link shows the female pelvic organs and the external genital anatomy. Preview it before class so you know what students will see.

Answer

  1. Put these in the order food passes through them: stomach, oesophagus, large intestine, small intestine, mouth, pharynx.
  2. Name the three parts of the small intestine. Which two can you find by name in the app?
  3. Trace urine from where it is made to where it leaves the body.
  4. About how long is the female urethra compared with the male one? Why does that matter for perineal and catheter care?

Activity 5. Skin and pressure injuries

Integumentary · about 15 minutes · Name: ____________________

Open

  • Open the apphttps://elvinpeters.com/projects/anatomy-sandbox/

Do

  1. Open the link. In Body systems, turn on Skin. It starts half see-through, so you can see the bones under it.
  2. Press Back, then Left, to look at the body as it lies on its back and on its side.
  3. Search and pick each one: Sacrum, Coccyx, Hip bone, Calcaneus, Scapula, Occipital bone, Femur, Ulna. On the hip bone, find the sitting bone at the bottom (the ischial tuberosity). On the femur, find the bump at the top outer edge (the greater trochanter). On the ulna, find the point of the elbow (the olecranon).

Answer

  1. Name five places where pressure injuries often form, and the bone under each one.
  2. Why do pressure injuries form over these spots?
