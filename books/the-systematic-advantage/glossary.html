Glossary

From The Systematic Advantage

The working vocabulary of The Systematic Advantage. Terms carried over from Volume 1 are marked (Vol 1). Where a term has a home chapter, it is noted so you can go deeper.

Accountability Map (Ch 14): A one-page written standard, one line per high-stakes output category, stating what you verify before that kind of output ships. It holds under deadline pressure and under negotiation because it was decided in advance.

Agentic workspace (Ch 9): An AI that can act rather than only answer: it opens and edits files, runs steps, produces working artifacts, and shows its work. The product-agnostic name for the category.

AI Champion (Ch 15): The person who builds a team's AI capability, distinct from being the team's AI expert. Four functions: translation, infrastructure building, practice modeling, and governance. The Champion succeeds by making themselves unnecessary for the basics.

AI Operating System (AI OS) (Ch 4): The persistent infrastructure a practitioner builds around their AI use: system instructions, context files, a prompt library, and workflow SOPs. The difference between a collection of prompts and an operating system is the difference between tools and infrastructure.

AI Task Matrix (Ch 2): The framework for deciding what to hand to AI and how much to verify, by scoring a task on what it is and what an error would cost.

Analysis Brief (Ch 11): The Professional Brief specialized for analysis work: the decision question, the audience, the input set with its limits and freshness, and the interpretation standard.

Architect's Mindset (Ch 1): The shift from working session by session to designing the system in which good sessions happen reliably. Volume 1's Director sets the vision; the Architect builds the environment.

Architecture Sprint (Ch 16): A ninety-day intensive for building a new infrastructure layer, as opposed to maintaining an existing one: a defined scope, a defined timeline (one design week, eight build weeks, three integration weeks), and a defined exit state.

Assumption Audit (Ch 7): A reasoning prompt that inventories what a plan depends on being true, rating each assumption's confidence and whether it has been tested.

Assumption Register (Ch 12): The strategy brief's distinguishing element: what a recommendation presupposes, ranked by impact if wrong, so the presuppositions get tested rather than buried.

Assumption Risk Matrix (Ch 12): The Assumption Register rated and sorted by likelihood-of-being-wrong against severity-if-wrong, critical assumptions first. It stops the audit from becoming philosophy by testing only what would change the recommendation.

Audience-First Brief (Ch 10): The Professional Brief rebuilt around a reader: recipient context, relationship dynamic, objective, tone register, and Voice Anchors. Communication is a briefing problem before it is a drafting problem.

Blindspot Pass (Ch 9): A pre-work prompt that collects the model's view of what you haven't asked for and what you've forgotten: the cheapest expertise in the session.

Calibrated trust (Ch 14): A practiced, domain-specific sense of where AI output is reliable and where it needs scrutiny, built from tracked experience rather than from confidence.

Constraint Hierarchy (Ch 5): Ranking a brief's constraints by whether their violation makes the output unusable (load-bearing) or merely imperfect (cosmetic), so the model spends effort where it matters.

Context contamination (Ch 8): Accumulated context that now pulls outputs in a direction you are no longer pursuing. The tell is a prompt that begins "ignore what we said earlier."

Context decay (Ch 8): The compound loss of working state (facts, decisions, vocabulary, calibration) between AI sessions.

Context window (Vol 1): The bounded working memory of a single AI session. Everything outside it does not exist to the model.

CRAFT (framework spine): Volume 2's operating cycle: Configure, Refine, Apply, Forward, Trust. The successor scaffold to Volume 1's CLEAR.

Decision Register (Ch 8): For long projects, a running record of every locked decision with its reasoning and later modifications, so the Handoff Protocol can point to it rather than compress it.

Director's Due Diligence (Ch 14): The high-stakes verification standard that extends Volume 1's Two-Step Verification Method for outputs with professional, legal, financial, or reputational consequences.

Domain calibration (Ch 14): The pattern knowledge a Personal Error Ledger produces: which output types in your specific field are reliable and which always need verification.

The effort dial (Ch 4): Matching the model tier and reasoning effort to the task rather than defaulting to the most powerful setting. A configuration knob rather than a framework.

Evidence-Weighted Output Model (Ch 14): Reviewing a high-stakes output claim by claim, sorting each into Tier 1 (verifiable facts, checked against a source), Tier 2 (reasoning, traced for logic), or Tier 3 (synthesis, weighed against judgment), and matching effort to tier.

Expert Panel (Ch 7): A reasoning prompt that assembles multiple disciplinary perspectives and asks them to surface their disagreements before any synthesis, so you get the real tensions rather than a diplomatic average.

Fresh-Start Rule (Ch 8): If correcting prior outputs has replaced advancing the work, close the session and reopen fresh with the current Handoff Protocol. You lose the contamination, never the content.

The Hardening Paradox (Ch 4): Hardened memory repeats whatever it holds, including mistakes. The harder the memory, the more expensive the error it carries, so standing memory needs scheduled rereads.

Instruction drift (Ch 4, Ch 6): A rule held in a prompt dilutes with every exchange until the model silently drops it. The cure is to move the rule to the persistent layer.

Interview Me (Ch 9): Flip the brief: the agent interviews you until the spec is complete, then plays it back. Extends Volume 1's Ask Me Questions.

Leadership Voice Brief (Ch 13): The Audience-First Brief extended for leadership: the five communication elements plus relationship history, organizational stakes, development intent, and the Voice Maintenance Protocol.

The Loop (Ch 9): The six-move method for a directed agentic session: Blindspot Pass, Interview Me, Anchor with References, Prototype Wide, Log the Deviations, Quiz Me. A loop is a workflow you run and rerun until it is reflex.

Memory hardening / the Memory Hardening Ladder (Ch 4): Moving context up the durability ladder, from memory that dies with a conversation to memory that survives sessions, weeks, and tool changes. The six rungs: naked chat, saved prompts, standing instructions, context files, working folder, self-maintaining system.

90-Day Team Adoption Plan (Ch 15): Three phases that move a team from broadcast to shared practice: Foundation (days 1–30), Expansion (31–60), and Integration (61–90).

Personal Error Ledger (Ch 14): A lightweight record of every AI output that required correction in a high-stakes context, with the error type. Sixty days of entries produce your calibration.

Pre-Mortem (Ch 7): A reasoning prompt that assumes a plan has already failed and asks for the specific mechanisms, so you get addressable pathways rather than abstract risk categories.

Professional Brief Model (Ch 5): The five-component brief that upgrades Volume 1's RTC: objective, context, ranked constraints, output format, and success criteria. The brief is the asset; the prompt is disposable.

Project Handoff Protocol (Ch 8): The five-field artifact produced at a session's end and consumed at the next session's opening: project snapshot, decisions locked with reasoning, open threads, next-session objective, and critical context.

Prototype Wide (Ch 9): Generating three or four genuinely different working versions before refining one. Exploration by artifact, where choosing beats generating.

Quarterly Practice Audit (Ch 16): The ninety-day, forty-five-minute self-assessment that prevents the plateau: score the five practice dimensions, flag outdated or missing infrastructure, and set a focused ninety-day plan on the two lowest dimensions. It is a design session rather than a grade.

Quiz Me (Ch 9): The session close where the agent quizzes you until you can explain every piece. You own what you can explain.

Red-Team Read (Ch 7): Having the AI attack your finished deliverable the way your toughest stakeholder would, before that stakeholder sees it.

The Re-Explanation Signal (Ch 4): The third time you type the same background paragraph into a fresh session, that paragraph is asking to become a file.

Reputational Audit (Ch 14): A six-question pre-send checklist that operationalizes Due Diligence: Tier 1 confirmed, Tier 2 traced, Tier 3 judged, high-stakes category reviewed by a second set of eyes, relationship-or-reputation risk considered, and finally, am I prepared to put my name on this.

Second-Opinion Run (Ch 14): Running the identical high-stakes brief on a second model and diffing the outputs. Agreement raises confidence; disagreement is a free risk detector pointing straight at the claim to verify.

Semantic drift (Ch 8): The gradual shift in how key terms are used across sessions, until outputs quietly contradict the original framing.

Socratic prompting (Ch 7): A two-step reasoning move: get the model's output, then ask it to argue against that output as rigorously as it can. The gap between the two is where the information that matters lives.

The Stale Check (Ch 11): Anything AI says about tools, prices, or versions carries an invisible expiry date. Two questions before a time-sensitive claim enters your work: how likely is this to be outdated, and how would I check.

Steelman Protocol (Ch 7): A pre-commitment reasoning practice: before finalizing a recommendation, assign the model to argue the strongest possible case against it, with no hedging and no restoring of your confidence at the end.

Strategy Brief (Ch 12): The Professional Brief specialized for strategy: the analysis brief's four elements plus a fifth, the Assumption Register.

The Synthesis Mindset (Ch 11): The habit of asking, after every synthesis output, "what does this mean, given what I know that this synthesis can't know?" The residual act that stays yours however complete the brief.

Team shared infrastructure (Ch 15): The floor a team builds together and each member builds above: a team prompt library that documents the missing context per entry, shared workflow SOPs, common system instructions, and a vocabulary document.

Terminology Lock (Ch 8): The Vocabulary Lock's client-grade tier: terms carrying legal, contractual, or client weight, whose misuse creates real downstream problems. When a term qualifies for both, the higher-stakes home wins.

Two-Step Verification Method (Vol 1): Classify an output as factual, subjective, or creative, then match your verification effort to the stakes.

Verification effort (Vol 1, extended Ch 14): The review work spent on an output. The skill is matching it to stakes rather than applying uniform effort everywhere.

Vocabulary Lock (Ch 8): A short glossary of the five to ten highest-weight working definitions for a project. It lives in the Architecture Prompt's vocabulary component; the Handoff Protocol carries only that session's new terms.

Voice Anchors (Ch 10): Two or three stable authorial patterns (how sentences open, whether asks hedge, the register between public and private) that make AI output read as authored. The one brief element no model can infer.

Voice Drift Check (Ch 13): A quarterly comparison of recent AI-assisted output against your Voice Anchor document, to catch the gradual homogenization toward AI's defaults.

Voice stewardship (Ch 13): The active practice of keeping AI-assisted output tied to your evolving voice: anchors, drift checks, and a maintenance cycle.

Workflow SOP (Ch 3): A documented, repeatable procedure for an AI-assisted workflow, written so a capable but non-expert colleague can run it: the steps, the AI handoff points, the quality check, and who reviews before deployment.

Terms in this glossary are drawn from the running series dictionary, which also seeds Volume 3's vocabulary. The practices that carry a worksheet or template are collected in the free companion Toolkit.
