From the talk: Get Real Work Out of AI

Brief it, fix it, check it.

The three cards from the talk. Keep them open next to your AI tool, print them, or copy the prompts. They're free and there is nothing to sign up for.

Card 1

The Brief

Most weak answers come from a thin request. Give every request three parts.

Role, Task, Constraint

  • Role: who the AI should act as, with the experience that matters for this job.
  • Task: the one thing you need, stated plainly. One deliverable per message.
  • Constraint: the guardrails. Length, tone, audience, format, and anything to leave out.

Example

Act as a senior HR manager with remote team experience. Write a 30-day onboarding checklist for a new remote employee in a client-facing role. Each item under 15 words, plain language, numbered list, no corporate jargon.

Two habits that help

  • Sharpen the task before you add to the role. A grand persona on a vague task still gets a vague answer.
  • Before a task outside your expertise, ask the AI what you're missing:
What questions would an expert in [field] ask that I probably haven't thought to ask yet?
Card 2

The Fix

"Make it better" is the slowest way to improve a draft. Diagnose it instead.

1. Name the symptom

Length, Tone, Structure, Specificity, or Accuracy.

2. Trace it to the part of the brief that caused it

  • Wrong tone usually means the Role never set one.
  • Generic or off-topic output usually means the Task was missing context or scope.
  • Too long, or the wrong shape, usually means a missing Constraint.

3. Send one targeted follow-up

The [symptom] isn't working. Specifically: [what's wrong]. Rewrite [section] to [fix]. Keep everything else as written.

When to start over

If you're four rounds in and it's still off, open a fresh chat with a better brief.

Before you close a long chat

Before we end this session, create a session summary for me to use when we continue. Include: (1) the goal of this project, (2) what we've accomplished today, (3) what's still outstanding, (4) the immediate next step when we resume, and (5) any important context I should include in my first message next session.
Card 3

The Check

AI sounds just as sure when it's wrong as when it's right. Check what matters, at the effort the stakes deserve.

Step 1: sort the claim

  • Fact: dates, statistics, quotes, named sources. Check these before you repeat them.
  • Judgment: recommendations and opinions. Treat them as a starting point and use your own judgment.
  • Creative: drafts, outlines, plans. Judge them by whether they fit and work.

Step 2: match your effort to the stakes

  • Low stakes: a quick look.
  • Medium stakes: a few minutes checking the one claim that would hurt most if it were wrong.
  • Legal, medical or financial decisions: confirm with a qualified person.

Paste this after any answer you plan to rely on

List every factual claim you just made as a numbered list. For each one, rate your confidence from 1 to 10, and tell me which ones I should verify from a primary source before repeating them. Flag anything you may have made up.

The rule

A citation counts once you've opened the page and read it yourself.
